Endothelial Regeneration Research

Experimental research investigating strategies to support endothelial cell recovery using photobiomodulation, biomimetic extracellular matrix substrates, and controlled HUVEC experiments.

Aug. 2025 – Present HUVEC Photobiomodulation Biomaterials

Research Overview

My undergraduate research focuses on endothelial cell recovery and regeneration. One portion of this work examines how red and blue light exposure interacts with biomimetic extracellular matrix substrates such as laminin and collagen to influence HUVEC recovery and growth.


The broader goal of the research is to understand how controlled physical and biochemical treatment conditions can support endothelial regeneration and contribute to the development of future therapeutic technologies.

Experimental Approach

Cell Model

Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) are used as the experimental endothelial cell model.

Photobiomodulation

Red and blue light exposures are evaluated as controlled treatment conditions for endothelial cell recovery.

ECM Substrates

Laminin and collagen are investigated as biomimetic extracellular matrix substrates for supporting cell attachment and recovery.

Selected Findings

The conference poster below presents results from the photobiomodulation and extracellular-matrix portion of the project. The study compared untreated cells, light-treated cells, ECM-coated conditions, and combined treatment groups.


The poster reports stronger HUVEC recovery under red-light conditions and shows particularly strong recovery when red-light exposure was combined with laminin.

Ongoing Research

The next phase of the project is investigating a glucose-based endothelial injury model. HUVECs are being exposed to elevated glucose conditions to induce cellular stress, after which regenerative treatment strategies will be evaluated using laminin, collagen, and red/blue light conditions.


Data collection for this phase is currently in progress, and results will be added once sufficient experimental data are available.


Additional work also includes evaluating gold nanoparticle biocompatibility and concentration limits for potential therapeutic-delivery applications.
